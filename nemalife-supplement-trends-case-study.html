Expert Article. Supplement Trends

Americans are taking more supplements than ever — and the basket looks nothing like it did.

A 25-year national analysis of 63,442 US adults shows supplement use climbing to 60% — but the real story is the rebalancing underneath: away from the old multivitamin, toward botanicals, biotics, and single-ingredient functionals.

01 — The Rebalancing

The multivitamin is fading. Everything else is rising.

Overall growth hides a category reshuffle. Multivitamin-multimineral use actually declined, while standalone vitamins, minerals, and botanicals taken outside a multivitamin all grew — and the share of adults taking four or more products nearly doubled.

03 — Fading Out

And the steepest declines tell their own story.

Two forces are clearing the shelf: ingredients pulled on safety (ephedra), and older botanicals whose efficacy stories never fully held up (ginseng, ginkgo, garlic). Several trace minerals dropped sharply as formulation practices changed. Each is a reminder that claims unsupported by biology eventually lose the consumer.

04 — Who's Buying

The 65-and-older shelf is now the biggest in the store.

Supplement use rose across every age band, but the steepest climb was among older adults — from 62% to 78%. The longevity and healthspan consumer is no longer a niche; it's the core market, and it's the most demanding on proof.

05 — The Pandemic Inflection

COVID rewired the immune-and-gut aisle.

Comparing the post-pandemic period (Aug 2021–Aug 2023) to just before it, products marketed for immunity, inflammation, and gut health jumped. These categories didn't just spike — they reset to a higher baseline, and they're squarely where consumers now expect evidence. Bars show the relative increase versus the pre-pandemic period.

The NemaLife Read

The market is diversifying toward exactly the ingredients that can't lean on legacy evidence.

Vitamin D and omega-3 have decades of human data. Ashwagandha, collagen, elderberry, functional mushrooms, prebiotics — the categories actually driving growth — largely don't. They're competing on consumer trust without the substantiation that older ingredients inherited.

  • Differentiation is the new battleground.When every brand sources the same trending botanical, biological proof is what separates a hero ingredient from a commodity.
  • Speed decides the window.Trends like elderberry and ashwagandha scaled faster than any human-trial timeline. Evidence has to arrive while the category is still open.
  • The buyer demands mechanism.An older, longevity-focused consumer — and the regulators behind them — want to know why it works, not just that a label says so.
